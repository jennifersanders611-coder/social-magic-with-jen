**AI made it possible for me to build software without writing code. It did not make it any easier to know what's worth building.**

I came across a LinkedIn post recently that made me laugh.

Someone joked that if every company starts vibe coding its own CRM, we're all going to have to learn a brand-new system every time we change jobs.

And I thought, well, that's a fair point.

Then I had another thought.

**Wait. I use Lovable. Am I part of the problem?**

Maybe. 😂

## What is vibe coding?

Vibe coding is using AI to build software by describing what you want instead of writing the code yourself.

You tell an AI tool what you're trying to create, and it writes the code.

Want a dashboard? Describe it.

Need a client portal? Explain what it should do.

Have an idea for a tool that doesn't exist? You might be able to build a working version before your next meeting.

Tools like Lovable have made software development accessible to people who aren't traditional developers.

People like me.

And I think that's pretty incredible.

## How I use Lovable as a growth marketer

I've used Lovable to turn ideas into working tools that make business information more accessible and useful.

Dashboards, reporting, data visualization, and systems that bring information together in ways that make sense.

Things that previously might have required a developer, a significant budget, or a much longer timeline.

Now I can experiment with an idea, see what works, and make improvements along the way.

As someone who works in growth marketing, that's exciting.

So much of my job involves connecting the dots between data, people, processes, and business opportunities.

Lovable gives me another way to do that.

And it's opened up an entirely new way of thinking about what's possible.

## AI is creating a new era of business software

For years, businesses have largely had two options.

Buy software that already exists and adapt their processes to fit it.

Or invest in custom development to create something specifically for their needs.

AI is introducing a third option.

What if you could create tools designed around how your business actually operates, without starting from scratch or needing a traditional development team?

That's a pretty significant shift.

And it has the potential to make technology more accessible to smaller businesses, marketers, operations teams, and people with great ideas who don't happen to know how to code.

I think that's something worth celebrating.

But it also raises some interesting questions.

## What happens when every company builds its own software?

This is where that LinkedIn post really got me thinking.

Imagine starting a new job and discovering that the company has its own custom-built CRM.

And its own project management system.

And its own reporting platform.

All designed specifically for that organization.

On one hand, that's amazing. Technology that fits the business instead of the other way around.

On the other hand, it changes how we think about software, training, support, and the skills employees need.

For years, knowing platforms like Salesforce, HubSpot, or Microsoft Dynamics has been a transferable professional skill.

But what happens when more businesses have their own systems?

Will understanding the underlying processes become more valuable than knowing a particular platform?

Will employees become more comfortable learning new technology because AI makes it easier to navigate?

Will the next generation of business software look completely different from what we're used to?

I don't know.

But I think those are fascinating questions.

## The hard part was never the code

Here's what I've learned from building with AI.

The tool can write the code in minutes.

It can't tell you what problem you're solving.

It can't tell you who the tool is for, what decision it should help someone make, or what "good" looks like when it's done.

That part is still on us.

AI is only as powerful as the clarity behind it. If your thinking is fuzzy, you'll just build a fuzzy tool faster.

## The opportunity is bigger than the technology

What excites me most about AI-powered development isn't the software itself.

It's the opportunity for people who understand their businesses to participate directly in creating solutions.

You don't have to be a developer to recognize an opportunity for improvement.

You don't have to know how to code to imagine a better customer experience.

And you don't have to wait for someone else to turn every idea into something tangible.

That doesn't mean technical expertise is no longer important. Security, reliability, integrations, and long-term maintenance still matter.

But the gap between having an idea and finding out whether it works has gotten dramatically smaller.

And that's a big deal.

## So, am I part of the problem?

Maybe I'm part of the change.

I genuinely love what tools like Lovable have made possible.

I love being able to experiment, create, and learn something new along the way.

And I love that people with business experience, creativity, and curiosity now have access to tools that once felt out of reach.

Will every company eventually have its own custom-built software?

Probably not.

Will AI change how we buy, build, and use business technology?

I think it already has.

And while I completely understand the guy who doesn't want to learn a brand-new CRM every time he changes jobs, I'm excited to see where this goes.

Vibe coding is changing who gets to build software.

But the people who build the best things won't be the ones who know the most about code.

They'll be the ones who are clearest
